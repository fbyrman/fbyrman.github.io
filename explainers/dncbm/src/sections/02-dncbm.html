2 Discover-then-Name CBMs

Discover-then-Name CBMs (DN-CBMs) (Rao et al., 2024) turn this around. Rather than fixing a concept set in advance and looking for it in images, they find the concepts a vision model already knows, then names them (Figure 2):

  1. Discover. A sparse autoencoder (Bricken et al., 2023) learns to reconstruct CLIP image embeddings through 8,192 mostly silent neurons. Its decoder is then dropped.
  2. Name. Each neuron gets the word, out of 20,000, whose CLIP text embedding is closest to its direction.
  3. Classify. A sparse linear probe maps the neurons to classes.

Figure 2. Top: the autoencoder is trained on reconstruction alone, without labels. Bottom: the trained encoder explaining a Places365 image; bars show each concept's contribution. Hover over a concept to find its neuron.
