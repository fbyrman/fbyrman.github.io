1 Contrastive learning

Vision-language models such as CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) learn from images and their web captions. Two encoders map images and text into one shared space, and training pulls each image towards its own caption and away from the others in the batch. Everything this contrastive learning needs is one matrix: the similarity of every image to every caption.

Figure 1. A batch of three pairs. Matches lie on the diagonal. Hover over a cell to see what it compares.

CLIP trains on this matrix with a softmax loss: each row is a multiple-choice question, which caption belongs to this image?, and each column the same question for a caption. With similarity $\kappa$ and temperature $\tau$:

\[ \mathcal{L}_\text{softmax} = -\frac{1}{2|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{B}|} \left[ \log \frac{e^{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_i)/\tau}}{\sum_{j} e^{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_j)/\tau}} + \log \frac{e^{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_i)/\tau}}{\sum_{j} e^{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_j, \mathbf{y}_i)/\tau}} \right]. \]

The sigmoid loss of SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) instead asks a yes-or-no question for every cell, is this a match?, with label $z_{ij} = +1$ on the diagonal and $-1$ elsewhere, temperature $t$ and bias $b$:

\[ \mathcal{L}_\text{sigmoid} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{B}|} \sum_{j=1}^{|\mathcal{B}|} \log \sigma\big( z_{ij} \, ( t\, \kappa(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{y}_j) + b ) \big), \qquad \sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}. \]

Softmax is relative: a caption only has to beat the others in its row. Sigmoid is absolute: every pair has to clear a threshold on its own (Figure 2).

Figure 2. One batch, scored by softmax (left) and sigmoid (right). Each cell is the predicted chance that photo and caption match; gold frames mark the true pairs. Moving one similarity shifts a whole row under softmax, but a single cell under sigmoid.
