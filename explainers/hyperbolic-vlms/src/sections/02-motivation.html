2 Motivation

Thanks to strong zero-shot classification and retrieval results, hyperbolic VLMs have taken off quickly. Their embedding space is also easy to read, with general concepts near the origin and specific ones further out, and new papers keep refining the losses and pushing the benchmarks. But being hyperbolic is a matter of degree. Curvature sets a length scale, and how much of the geometry a model actually uses depends on where its embeddings sit relative to that scale. Both are learned, so nothing forces a model to go far enough out for the curvature to matter. If we want to explain why these models work, we need to know how hyperbolic they really are, a question that has so far received little attention. That is what our new paper does, by looking at the exponential map: the one place where curvature enters an otherwise Euclidean network.
